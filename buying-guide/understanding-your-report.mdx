A Nestlyze property analysis report is a structured document, not a wall of AI-generated text. It has distinct sections in a deliberate order: the composite verdict first, then the Nestimate valuation, then the 6 domain deep-dives, then an actionable next-steps checklist. Each section answers a different question. This guide walks through every one of them.

## The composite score

The score at the top of every report is a number from 0 to 100. It's a weighted average of the 6 domain scores, synthesized by a separate AI layer that accounts for how severe each domain's findings are — not just their individual numbers.

| Score range | What it means |
|---|---|
| 85–100 | Exceptional — standout signals across domains; due-diligence items are routine confirmations |
| 75–84 | Solid — no material concerns; standard due diligence remains |
| 65–74 | Worth pursuing with specific checks — one or two confirmed concerns to price in or verify |
| 50–64 | Real concerns — multiple domains flag material issues; read carefully before offering |
| Below 50 | Significant red flags — serious confirmed issues; proceed only if you understand each trade-off |

The score is displayed alongside a radar chart that shows each domain's contribution, so you can see at a glance whether a low overall score is driven by one bad domain or spread across several.

<Note>
  If your Nestlyze profile is complete, the composite score is personalized — it's weighted to your criteria. A buyer who needs a top-rated school district will see school quality weighted higher than it would be for a buyer who listed schools as unimportant. The report marks personalized scores with a purple badge.
</Note>

## The deal-breaker banner

Certain findings cap the composite score at 40, regardless of how every other domain scores. These hard stops exist for confirmed deal-killers only:

- **Title defects** — unresolved liens, boundary disputes, or clouded title that make the property difficult or impossible to sell
- **Code violations** — open permits or unpermitted additions flagged by public records that affect habitability or insurability
- **HOA insolvency** — an HOA with a reserve fund so depleted that a special assessment is likely or already levied

If a deal-breaker banner appears, the report explains exactly what triggered it and links to the relevant section. A capped score of 40 doesn't mean the property is worthless — it means you have a specific problem to resolve before proceeding.

<Warning>
  A deal-breaker flag represents a confirmed finding from public records, not speculation. Investigate the specific issue with your buyer's agent and a real estate attorney before making any offer.
</Warning>

## The 6 domain cards

Each domain runs as an independent AI agent using its own data sources, then reports back its score, top findings, pros, cons, and risk flags. The synthesizer aggregates all six. Here's what each domain covers:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Financial — total cost of ownership">
    The Financial agent models what this home will actually cost you over time, not just the monthly mortgage payment. It covers:

    - **Mortgage scenarios** — monthly payment breakdowns for four down-payment scenarios (FHA 3.5%, conventional 10%, conventional 20%, VA 0%), including principal and interest, PMI if applicable, estimated property tax, homeowners insurance, HOA, and a 1% annual maintenance reserve
    - **System replacement projections** — every major home system (roof, HVAC, water heater, electrical panel, windows, appliances) is assessed against the property's age. A 1998 home with its original HVAC is approaching the end of an 18-year typical lifespan; the report flags this as a near-term budget item
    - **Tax reassessment** — under California's Prop 13, property tax resets to approximately 1.22% of the purchase price when a property changes hands. If the previous owner bought in 1995, the current tax bill will be a fraction of yours. Expect a supplemental tax bill 4–6 months after closing
    - **Insurance estimates** — baseline homeowners insurance (~0.55% of home value annually in California after the 2023–25 market reset) plus wildfire, flood, and earthquake supplements where applicable
    - **Listing signal scanner** — the agent reads the listing description for red flags ("as-is," "investor special," "needs TLC") and green flags ("new roof," "owned solar," "permitted addition")
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Market — comparable sales and price trends">
    The Market agent answers the core valuation question: is the asking price reasonable? It covers:

    - **Comparable sales (comps)** — recent closed transactions for similar homes within a defined radius, adjusted for square footage, age, and condition
    - **Days on market** — how long the listing has been active relative to the local median. A home sitting 60+ days in a market where the median is 14 suggests something is wrong with the price, condition, or both
    - **Price trend** — whether the neighborhood's median sale price is rising, flat, or declining over the trailing 6 and 12 months
    - **List-to-sale ratio** — whether homes in this area typically sell above or below asking, which directly informs your offer anchor

    The Market agent's findings feed directly into the [offer strategy](/buying-guide/offer-strategy) section of the report.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Physical — structure, systems, and condition">
    The Physical agent assesses the observable condition of the property using listing photos, street view imagery, and satellite analysis processed through AI vision. It covers:

    - **Roof condition** — visible damage, age signals, moss or debris accumulation
    - **Exterior condition** — paint, siding, gutters, drainage, driveway
    - **Foundation and structure** — visible cracks, settling signals, grading issues that direct water toward the foundation
    - **Interior signals** — photo analysis of kitchen, bathrooms, flooring, and ceilings for evidence of water damage, deferred maintenance, or recent renovation
    - **HVAC and mechanical** — age and condition estimates where information is available

    The Physical agent cannot see inside walls. Its findings are based on available visual information and listing disclosures. Use a licensed home inspector during the contingency period to verify or deepen every Physical agent finding.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Environmental — flood, fire, earthquake, and air">
    The Environmental agent pulls from four external data sources automatically for every analysis:

    - **FEMA flood map** — the property's flood zone designation (AE, AO, A, VE = Special Flood Hazard Area requiring mandatory flood insurance for federally-backed mortgages; Zone X = minimal risk). Flood insurance in a SFHA zone runs $700–$5,000/year
    - **Cal Fire wildfire risk** — the state's official fire hazard severity zone classification. In high-risk zones, budget for FAIR Plan coverage — many major insurers (State Farm, Allstate, USAA) stopped writing new policies in California fire zones after 2023
    - **USGS seismic hazard** — earthquake ground-shaking potential based on USGS maps. California Earthquake Authority (CEA) insurance runs approximately $0.20 per $100 of home value annually, with a high deductible (10–25%)
    - **EPA Superfund proximity** — any EPA-designated hazardous contamination sites within 5 miles. Being within 1 mile of a Superfund site can reduce property value 5–15% and raises health concerns

    <Note>
      If USGS or FEMA data sources are temporarily unavailable during your analysis, the report generates with that section marked as unavailable and no credit is deducted. The report will note which specific data source was offline.
    </Note>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Neighborhood — schools, crime, walkability">
    The Neighborhood agent covers location quality at the street level:

    - **Schools** — Nestlyze uses school-attendance-boundary polygons (not "nearest school" approximations) to confirm the exact elementary, middle, and high school that serves the specific address. The GreatSchools rating (1–10) is included. A one-block difference can put an address in a different district
    - **Walk Score** — a 0–100 measure of how many daily errands you can accomplish on foot. In the Bay Area, every 10 points of Walk Score correlates with meaningful home value differences. Below 50 means car-dependent; above 70 is very walkable
    - **Crime** — FBI UCR data at the census tract level, covering property crime and violent crime rates relative to the national median
    - **Commute signals** — BART station distance for Bay Area properties, and commute-time estimates to destinations you specified in your profile

    All Neighborhood analysis cites only housing factors (school rating, crime rate, walkability, commute distance). Nestlyze explicitly prohibits its models from reasoning over demographic proxies or protected-class characteristics.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Legal — zoning, HOA, and permits">
    The Legal agent surfaces the issues that don't show up in listing photos but can derail a purchase or become expensive post-close:

    - **Zoning** — whether the property's current use is consistent with its zoning classification, and whether any planned changes to the parcel's zoning are on record
    - **Permits** — open permits, expired permits, or unpermitted additions flagged in public records. Unpermitted work can affect the appraisal, prevent refinancing, and create liability at resale
    - **HOA health** — for properties in a homeowners association, the agent reviews available reserve fund data, pending special assessments, and litigation history. An HOA with a reserve fund below 10% of required reserves is a financial risk for all members
    - **Title signals** — liens, easements, or encumbrances that appear in public records and should be reviewed by a title company before close
    - **Rent control and tenant status** — for tenant-occupied properties, applicable rent control ordinances and estimated relocation costs

    Legal findings from the report are starting points for review, not legal advice. Engage a real estate attorney if the Legal agent surfaces anything material.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## The Nestimate valuation card

Nestimate is Nestlyze's glass-box valuation — a price range with honest confidence, not a single black-box number. It appears as its own card above the domain deep-dives.

The card shows:
- **Low–High range** — the valuation range based on comparable sales, property condition signals, listing photos processed through vision AI, and school quality
- **Confidence tier** — High, Medium, or Low, depending on how much hard data was available (comp density, photo quality, public records completeness)
- **Delta from asking** — whether the asking price falls within, above, or below the Nestimate range, expressed as a percentage
- **Reasoning bullets** — 5–8 specific factors driving the number (comp analysis, condition adjustments, school premium, days-on-market signal, etc.)
  - **Model details** — Nestimate uses a statistical comp engine that finds nearest neighbors by location and property type within 5 miles and 12 months. It falls back to an AI reasoning approach when comps are too sparse

On a real 2022 sale (Newberry, FL — listed $556K, sold $550,912), the Nestimate prediction was $545K — 1.1% off. Nestlyze publishes both accurate and inaccurate cases.

<Tip>
  Use the Nestimate range to anchor your offer, not as a floor or ceiling. A home priced 8% above the Nestimate high is worth investigating before offering at asking. See [offer strategy](/buying-guide/offer-strategy) for how to translate this into a specific offer price.
</Tip>

## The executive summary and next steps

Below the domain cards, the report has two final sections:

**Executive summary** — a synthesized narrative written by a separate AI layer that reads all six domain reports and produces a plain-English summary of the most important findings. It leads with the single most important thing to know about the property, then covers the key concerns and strengths.

**Next steps** — an ordered checklist of specific actions tailored to the findings. These are not generic ("get a home inspection") — they're specific to what the agents found ("ask the listing agent about the open permit for the 2018 addition" or "verify the sewer lateral condition — this is a 1962 home with no mention of sewer replacement in the listing").

The due-diligence checklist uses three priority levels:
- **Critical** — confirmed deal-killers or issues with major financial consequence
- **High** — serious concerns that require verification before making an offer
- **Check** — items to confirm but unlikely to be blocking issues

## The Ask AI widget

Every report includes an Ask AI widget at the bottom. Use it to ask follow-up questions about the specific findings — "what does a Zone AE flood designation mean for insurance costs on this property?" or "is the Mello-Roos on this property typical for this ZIP code?" The widget uses the same Claude models as the domain agents and has access to the full report context. Ask AI queries do not consume additional credits.
