Getting an offer accepted requires more than finding a number the seller will say yes to. In competitive Bay Area markets, your offer is a package — price, earnest money, contingency terms, close-of-escrow date, and in some cases a personal letter. The Nestlyze property analysis gives you the data layer. This guide explains how to translate it into a specific, defensible bid.

## Using Nestimate to anchor your price

The Nestimate card in your report shows a valuation range (low–high) with a confidence tier. Start your pricing process here.

**If the asking price falls within the Nestimate range:** The home is priced reasonably. In a balanced market, consider offering at or slightly below asking. In a hot market (median days on market below 14), plan to offer at or above asking and bid against the Nestimate mid-point, not the asking price.

**If the asking price is above the Nestimate high:** The home is priced above what comparable sales justify. You have three choices: (1) offer at the Nestimate high and accept that your offer may be passed over, (2) offer at asking but structure an appraisal-gap clause so you're only partially exposed, or (3) pass and wait. A home priced 8%+ above Nestimate often comes back to market.

**If the asking price is below the Nestimate low:** The price signals something — a motivated seller, a condition issue the analysis flagged, or a competitive listing strategy designed to trigger multiple offers. In the last case, expect to offer above asking. Use the Market agent's list-to-sale ratio to gauge by how much homes in this neighborhood typically go over.

<Tip>
  Use the Nestimate confidence tier to calibrate how firmly to anchor. A "High" confidence rating means there were enough comparable sales and clean data to produce a tight range — treat it as a reliable anchor. A "Low" confidence rating means sparse comps or limited data; in that case widen your mental range by 5–8% in each direction.
</Tip>

## Reading the Market agent's comparable sales section

The Market agent in your report shows the recent closed transactions the valuation is based on. Open this section before writing your offer. Key things to look for:

**Price per square foot spread.** If the comps range from $650–$900/sqft, a home at $950/sqft is priced for a bidding war or has a premium feature the comps don't. If the subject home is asking $600/sqft against $750–$900 comps, find out why before assuming it's a deal.

**Sale date of comps.** Comps from 18 months ago in a market that moved 10% in either direction are misleading. The Market agent flags comp age — if the freshest comps are 12+ months old, add a wider margin of uncertainty to the Nestimate.

**Days on market for the subject property.** The OfferCheatSheetCard in your report uses the listing's days on market (DOM) to calibrate the offer envelope:

- **Under 14 DOM:** Hot — plan for multiple offers; earnest money at 3%; consider pre-offer inspection
- **14–60 DOM:** Balanced — standard contingency periods; earnest money at 2%
- **Over 60 DOM:** Soft — the seller is under pressure; keep all contingencies; earnest money at 1%

## The Offer Cheat Sheet card

Inside the Nestlyze report, below the Market agent section, the Offer Cheat Sheet surfaces the questions first-time buyers always ask the night before signing: how much earnest money, which contingencies to keep, and what cash to bring to closing.

**Earnest money guidance:**
- 1–3% of purchase price is the typical range in the Bay Area
- 3–5% is common in hot markets as a signal of seriousness
- The deposit is held in escrow and applied to your down payment at closing
- It's fully refundable if you cancel within a contingency period
- Once all contingencies are removed, the deposit is at risk — you forfeit it if you walk

**Cash to close reminder:**
Down payment plus closing costs (typically 2–3% of purchase price) must arrive by wire 1–2 days before the signing appointment. Never confirm wire instructions by email alone — call the title company directly using a number from their official website.

## When to keep contingencies vs. when to waive

Contingencies protect you. Waiving them transfers financial risk onto you permanently — there's no reinstating a contingency once it's been removed.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Inspection contingency">
    **Standard period:** 7–17 days depending on the contract.

    **Keep it in a balanced or soft market.** Even a pristine-looking home hides $5,000–$15,000 of average deferred maintenance (sewer lines, HVAC wear, roof near end of life). A full home inspection runs $300–$600 and is the best-value due-diligence dollar you'll spend.

    **In a hot market:** Consider a pre-offer inspection. Pay for a licensed inspector to walk the property before you submit the offer, then structure your offer without an inspection contingency — but informed by an actual inspection. This is different from waiving cold. A pre-offer inspection takes 2–3 hours and costs $300–$600, but it lets you bid without the contingency while still knowing what you're buying.

    <Warning>
      Waiving the inspection contingency without a pre-offer inspection means you cannot back out if the post-offer inspection uncovers $50,000 of foundation damage. This risk is real. If Nestlyze's Physical agent flagged structural concerns or the listing is marked "as-is," do not waive this contingency.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Appraisal contingency">
    **Standard period:** 17–21 days.

    **Keep it unless you have substantial reserves.** The appraisal contingency lets you cancel if the lender's appraisal comes in below the purchase price. Without it, you're contractually obligated to close even if the appraisal is $150,000 below what you offered.

    **In a hot market, consider an appraisal-gap clause instead of outright waiving.** An appraisal-gap clause says you'll cover the gap between the appraised value and the purchase price up to a defined limit (e.g., "buyer will cover up to $75,000 below appraised value"). This is more seller-friendly than a full contingency and less risky than waiving entirely. It works best when you have 25%+ down — the loan-to-value ratio makes the lender comfortable even with a gap.

    **Only waive the appraisal contingency if** you have all-cash or enough reserves to bridge a worst-case appraisal gap of 10–15%.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Financing contingency">
    **Standard period:** 21 days.

    **Keep this one.** Always. Even in the hottest markets, waiving the financing contingency means you owe the purchase price even if your loan falls through for any reason. Lenders pull final approval through underwriting, employment verification, and asset verification, all of which can fail at the last minute. Even clean loan files have a 10–15% fall-through rate industry-wide.

    **The only exception:** paying cash. If there's no loan, there's nothing to protect.

    <Warning>
      Sellers and their agents will sometimes pressure buyers to waive the financing contingency to strengthen an offer. The risk is entirely on you. If your loan falls through after waiving, you lose your earnest money and potentially face a lawsuit for the purchase price difference.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Seller questions section

The Nestlyze report includes a list of specific questions to ask the listing agent before or during the offer process. These come from the Legal, Physical, and Financial agents' findings — not generic boilerplate. For example:

- "The public record shows an open permit for a 2019 bathroom addition — can you provide the finaled permit?"
- "The listing notes a solar lease — can you share the remaining term, monthly payment, and buyout cost?"
- "The HOA reserve fund study is listed as pending — what is the current reserve balance?"

Bring this list to every tour and to the disclosure review. The answers you get — or don't get — are data.

## Offer checklist

<Steps>
  <Step title="Run the Nestlyze analysis before writing the offer">
    Confirm the composite score, review the Nestimate range, check the Market agent's comps, and read the Legal agent findings for any title or permit issues.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Establish your price anchor from Nestimate">
    Note whether the asking price is inside, above, or below the Nestimate range, and the Nestimate confidence tier.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check days on market to calibrate earnest money and contingency posture">
    Under 14 DOM → hot strategy. 14–60 DOM → balanced. Over 60 DOM → soft market, keep all contingencies.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Determine contingency strategy with your buyer's agent">
    Decide which contingencies to keep, whether a pre-offer inspection makes sense, and whether an appraisal-gap clause is appropriate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm your cash-to-close number">
    Down payment + estimated closing costs + earnest money deposit. Verify funds are accessible and not in a vehicle that takes time to liquidate (retirement accounts, brokerage positions).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the seller questions list from the report">
    Ask each question to the listing agent before signing. Answers not provided before offer acceptance become items to investigate during the inspection contingency period.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit the offer with your buyer's agent">
    Your agent handles the CAR RPA contract form and negotiation. The Nestlyze data is your due-diligence layer — bring it to every conversation.
  </Step>
</Steps>
