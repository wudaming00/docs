The listing price is the beginning of the financial conversation, not the end. Between the down payment, closing costs, first-year repairs, and ongoing ownership expenses, the cash you need to comfortably buy a home is often 30–40% more than the down payment alone. The Nestlyze Financial agent models all of these — but understanding what each category means helps you pressure-test those numbers against your own situation.

## Upfront costs

These are the dollars you need before you get the keys.

### Down payment

Your down payment is a percentage of the purchase price that you pay in cash at closing. The loan covers the rest.

| Loan type | Minimum down payment | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional | 5–20% | Below 20% triggers PMI; most competitive Bay Area offers put down 20%\+ |
| FHA | 3.5% | Requires mortgage insurance for the life of the loan if you put down less than 10% |
| VA | 0% | For qualifying veterans and active service members only; no PMI |
| Jumbo | 10–30% | Bay Area purchases above the conforming loan limit (\$766,550 in most CA counties) often require 20–30% |

On a $1.5M Bay Area home at 20% down, the down payment is $300,000. At 10% down, it's $150,000 — but you'll pay PMI of approximately $625–\$1,250/month (0.5–1% of the loan amount annually) until you reach 20% equity.

<Note>
  In competitive Bay Area markets, putting down less than 20% often signals a financially stretched buyer to the seller. If you're competing against offers with 20%\+ down, a lower down payment can cost you the house even if your offer price is identical.
</Note>

### Earnest money deposit

When your offer is accepted, you typically wire 1–3% of the purchase price into escrow within 3 business days as an earnest money deposit. In hot markets, buyers sometimes put up 3–5% as a credibility signal.

This money is not an additional cost — it's applied toward your down payment at closing. But it's at risk: if you cancel after all contingencies are removed, you forfeit it. On a $1.5M home at 2%, that's $30,000 at stake the moment contingencies are released.

### Home inspection

A licensed inspector spends 2–4 hours examining the visible and accessible systems of the property. They check the roof, attic, foundation, structure, electrical panel, plumbing, HVAC, and interior surfaces.

**Typical cost:** $300–$**600** for a standard Bay Area single-family home. Larger homes, older homes, or inspections that include add-ons (sewer lateral, roof specialist, chimney) run higher.

A sewer lateral inspection (a camera run through the lateral from the house to the street) adds $150–$300 and is strongly recommended for homes built before 1980. Cast iron pipes corrode; Orangeburg pipes (used in some 1940s–1960s homes) fail. Sewer replacement runs $5,000–$15,000.

### Appraisal

Your lender orders and pays for the appraisal as part of the loan process, but the cost is typically passed to you in closing costs or charged upfront.

**Typical cost:** $500–$**900** for a standard single-family home. Complex properties, multi-unit buildings, or high-value homes requiring additional comparable sales work can run $1,000–$1,500.

If the appraisal comes in below the purchase price, you either need to renegotiate with the seller, cover the gap in cash, or exercise your appraisal contingency to exit the deal.

## Closing costs

Closing costs are due at the close-of-escrow date, on top of your down payment. They cover the third-party services required to transfer ownership of the property.

**Typical range: 2–5% of purchase price.** On a $1.5M Bay Area home, expect $30,000–\$75,000 in closing costs.

## Ongoing ownership costs

These costs begin the month you close and continue for as long as you own the property.

### Property tax

In California, property tax is governed by Proposition 13: the assessed value resets to the purchase price when the property changes hands, and then increases by no more than 2% per year until the next sale.

**Effective tax rates by Bay Area county:**

| County | Effective rate |
| --- | --- |
| Santa Clara | ~1.25% |
| Alameda | ~1.35% |
| San Mateo | ~1.18% |
| San Francisco | ~1.15% |
| Contra Costa | ~1.22% |
| Marin | ~1.10% |

On a $1.5M San Jose home (Santa Clara County), annual property tax runs approximately $18,750 — or \$1,563/month added to your mortgage payment.

**Prop 13 shock:** If the previous owner bought the same home in 1995 for $300,000, their annual tax bill is approximately $3,750. After you purchase at \$1.5M, the tax resets to your purchase price. Expect a supplemental tax bill — covering the gap between the old assessment and the new one for the remaining portion of the fiscal year — to arrive 4–6 months after closing.

### Mello-Roos (California only)

Mello-Roos is a California special tax district assessment that funds public infrastructure (schools, roads, parks, utilities) in newer developments. It's levied under the Mello-Roos Community Facilities Act of 1982, which means it primarily affects homes built in planned communities after that year.

**Cost range:** $2,000–$**8,000\+ per year**, often lasting 25–40 years from the district's formation. It appears as a "CFD" (Community Facilities District) line item on your property tax bill.

Mello-Roos is not reflected in the 1.25% base rate above — it's additive. A $4,000/year Mello-Roos on a home with an already-$18,750 property tax bill brings your annual tax cost to \$22,750.

Check for Mello-Roos before making an offer: look up the address on your county assessor's website, or ask the listing agent for the current tax bill. The Nestlyze Financial agent flags Mello-Roos when public records indicate a CFD.

### Private mortgage insurance (PMI)

If you put down less than 20% on a conventional loan, your lender requires PMI — insurance that protects the lender (not you) if you default.

**Cost: 0.5–1.5% of the loan amount annually.** On a $1.2M loan (20% down on a $1.5M home avoids PMI; 10% down leaves a $1.35M loan), PMI runs $562–\$1,688/month.

PMI cancels automatically once your loan balance reaches 78% of the original purchase price (for conforming loans). You can request cancellation when you reach 80% equity with evidence of current home value.

### HOA dues

Homeowners association dues cover shared amenity maintenance, building insurance (for condos and townhomes), exterior maintenance, landscaping, and reserves. In the Bay Area:

- **Standalone single-family homes in an HOA:** $100–$600/month
- **Townhomes:** $300–$800/month
- **Condos:** $400–$1,200\+/month (building insurance is included in condo HOA dues)

Before buying any HOA property, request the HOA's most recent reserve fund study, the last 12 months of meeting minutes, and the CC&Rs. An HOA with a reserve fund below 10% of required reserves is likely to levy a special assessment — a one-time charge to all members that can run $5,000–$30,000 with little warning.

### Homeowners insurance

**Baseline cost in California after the 2023–25 market reset:** approximately 0.55–0.65% of home value annually for a standard market home — roughly $8,250–$9,750/year on a $1.5M home, or $690–\$812/month.

This reflects the post-2023 repricing driven by State Farm, Allstate, and USAA withdrawing from parts of the California market. The pre-2023 benchmark (0.35–0.40%) systematically undercounts current rates.

**Supplemental insurance to budget for:**

| Coverage type | Annual cost |
| --- | --- |
| Flood (NFIP, SFHA zones) |  |
| Wildfire (CA FAIR Plan in high-risk zones) | ~1.5x normal homeowners — major insurers no longer writing in these areas |
| Earthquake (CEA) | ~0.20% of home value; 10–25% deductible |

For a home in a high wildfire severity zone, the CA FAIR Plan is often the only option — combined with a DIC (Difference in Conditions) wrap policy for coverage the FAIR Plan doesn't include. Budget 2–3x the standard homeowners insurance rate for fire-zone properties.

### Maintenance and repairs

The **1% rule** is a starting point: budget 1% of the home's value per year for routine maintenance and unexpected repairs. On a $1.5M home, that's $15,000/year or \$1,250/month.
